# Anti-Slop Writing

## Goal

Improve clarity and naturalness without changing the underlying facts, confidence level, or meaning.

## Remove these failure modes

### 1. Empty significance

Avoid inflating ordinary observations into sweeping claims about importance, transformation, the future, or society unless the evidence supports them.

### 2. Stock framing

Use plain language instead of repeated templates such as:

- "It is important to note..."
- "In today's rapidly changing world..."
- "This is not just X; it is Y."
- "A key takeaway is..."
- "Let us delve into..."
- generic "Bottom Line / Significance / Perspective" headings

These phrases are not forbidden because of a blacklist. Remove them when they add framing rather than information.

### 3. Artificial contrast

Do not invent an opposing idea merely to create rhetorical shape. Say the actual relationship directly.

### 4. Formulaic paragraphs

Avoid making every paragraph follow the same pattern: topic sentence → three points → "therefore" conclusion.

Vary sentence length and paragraph structure according to the material.

### 5. Synonym inflation

Do not replace simple words with grander words just to sound sophisticated. Prefer the precise word that a competent human writer would naturally choose.

### 6. Repetition

Remove repeated claims, repeated conclusions, and repeated transitions even when the wording differs.

### 7. Fake specificity

Do not invent examples, labels, statistics, observations, citations, or technical terms to make prose feel concrete.

## Academic exception

Do not "humanize" by removing precision from academic or technical writing. Technical terminology is appropriate when it carries real meaning.

## Preserve

- user facts
- numbers
- citations
- uncertainty
- hedging where warranted
- domain-specific terminology
- requested structure

## Final test

Ask:

> If I deleted this sentence, would the reader lose information, reasoning, evidence, or a necessary transition?

If the answer is no, cut or shorten it.
