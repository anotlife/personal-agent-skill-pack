# Academic Register

## Principle

Academic writing has conventions that are **not** AI tells. Removing them does not make writing more human; it makes it less precise. The task is to remove padding, not formality.

## Keep

| Convention | Why it stays |
|---|---|
| Domain terminology | It carries precise meaning that no synonym preserves |
| Passive voice when the process matters | "Samples were incubated at 37 °C for 24 h" — the actor is irrelevant |
| Formal connectives used correctly | "therefore", "in contrast", "given that" carry real logical content |
| Required structure | The assignment's headings, order, and sections |
| Equations, units, statistical vocabulary | They are the content |
| Hedging that reflects real uncertainty | "consistent with", "not detectable at this n" |
| Third person where the discipline requires it | Convention, not evasion |
| Citations in the required style | Formatting is part of the register |

## Remove

| Pattern | Why |
|---|---|
| Decorative filler ("It is important to note that") | Contains no claim |
| Inflated significance ("groundbreaking", "revolutionary") | Claims more than the evidence |
| Generic historical background | Not needed to understand this experiment |
| Repeated transitions ("Furthermore, ... Moreover, ...") | The relationship is usually just "and" |
| Unsupported significance paragraphs | No evidence attaches to them |
| Redundant definitions of well-known terms | The reader is a marker, not a novice |
| "In conclusion" paragraphs restating the introduction | Zero new information |
| Casual synonyms replacing technical terms | Destroys precision |

## The register test

Ask: *would a marker mistake this sentence for padding?* If yes, cut it. Then ask: *would a marker mistake this sentence for imprecision?* If yes, restore the technical term.

These two tests pull in opposite directions, and the tension is the whole job.

## Concrete before abstract

| Abstract | Concrete |
|---|---|
| "The system exhibited improved performance characteristics" | "Median response time fell from 240 ms to 95 ms" |
| "An investigation of the factors was undertaken" | "We tested three temperatures" |
| "The results were found to be significant" | "The difference was significant (t(8) = 3.1, p = 0.015)" |
| "Various challenges were encountered" | "The sensor drifted by 4% per hour during the 6-hour run" |

## Passive voice: keep it when the process is the subject

Correct passive: "The solution was heated to 60 °C." The actor is irrelevant, and the passive keeps attention on the process.

Incorrect passive (the kind that gets flagged): "It was determined that an improvement was observed." No actor, no agent, no process — just an abstract statement of an abstract statement. Rewrite: "Output rose 12%."

The distinguishing question is not active vs passive but: *does the sentence have a subject doing something?*

## Hedging that is part of the register

Do not "de-AI" these away:

- "The result is consistent with X" (correct for a correlation)
- "No effect was detectable at n = 6" (correct for a null result)
- "The mechanism remains unclear" (correct when it is unclear)
- "This suggests, but does not establish, ..." (correct)

They are the substance of careful writing. Removing them to sound more confident is a correctness regression.

## Formatting conventions

- Spell out an acronym at first use, then use it.
- Define symbols at first use.
- Use the tense convention of the discipline: past tense for what was done and observed, present tense for what is generally true.
- Numbers: numerals for measurements, spelled out only where convention requires.
- Do not use contractions in formal sections; do not avoid them artificially in notes.
- Avoid the first person unless the discipline and the assignment allow it, or unless describing a choice ("we chose X because Y") where it clarifies.

## Checklist

- [ ] No padding sentence survived the "would a marker call this filler" test.
- [ ] No technical term was replaced with a casual synonym.
- [ ] Passive voice appears only where the process, not the actor, is the subject.
- [ ] Hedges that carry real uncertainty are intact.
- [ ] Acronyms and symbols are defined at first use.
- [ ] Tense follows the discipline's convention.
