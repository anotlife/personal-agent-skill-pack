# Causal Reasoning

A causal claim needs more than temporal sequence or correlation.

Ask:

1. What changed?
2. What outcome changed?
3. What alternative causes changed at the same time?
4. Is there a control, comparison, repeated observation, or natural experiment?
5. Could selection, measurement, regression to the mean, confounding, or reverse causality explain the pattern?
6. Is the proposed mechanism directly measured or merely plausible?

## Safe ladder

- “X and Y were observed together.”
- “The result is consistent with X affecting Y.”
- “The evidence supports X as one contributor.”
- reserve strong causal language for designs that warrant it.
