# Inference Failure Catalog

Each entry has the breakage and the **repair** — the sentence you write instead of the fallacy's name.

## Evidence-to-claim failures

**Correlation as causation.** Two variables moved together; the argument concludes one drives the other.
*Repair:* name the third variables that changed at the same time, and state the association without the causal verb.

**Small sample generalised.** n = 5 becomes "most people".
*Repair:* report the actual n and the actual proportion. "All five respondents preferred X."

**Selected examples as representative.** The author picked cases that support the conclusion.
*Repair:* ask how the cases were selected. State whether the sample could have shown the opposite result.

**Survivorship / selection effect.** Only the successes are observable.
*Repair:* ask what happened to the cases that were not observed, and whether they would change the conclusion.

**Base-rate neglect.** A rate is presented without the underlying frequency.
*Repair:* add the base rate. "Of 1,000 tested, 50 had it" is a different statement from "5% have it" without the population.

**Anecdote as prevalence evidence.** One story stands in for a rate.
*Repair:* label it as a single case and say what measurement would give a rate.

**Absence of evidence as evidence of absence.** "No study has found X, therefore X does not occur."
*Repair:* state the power of the search. "No study found X at the sample sizes used; small effects are not excluded."

**Appeal to an unspecified authority.** "Experts agree" with no citation, or a citation to a summary of a summary.
*Repair:* name the source and what it actually measured, or drop the support.

**Unfalsifiable claim.** Nothing could contradict it.
*Repair:* ask what observation would count against the claim. If nothing, restate it as a definition or a value judgement.

## Logical failures

**False dichotomy.** Two options presented where more exist.
*Repair:* name a third option, or say the options are exhaustive under a stated constraint.

**Equivocation.** A key term shifts meaning mid-argument.
*Repair:* write the two definitions and show where the shift happens.

**Circularity.** The conclusion is a premise in disguise.
*Repair:* state the conclusion as a premise and show that it is doing all the work.

**Motte-and-bailey.** A modest claim is defended while a strong claim is argued.
*Repair:* separate the two and audit the strong one.

**Strawman.** A weaker version of the opposing claim is attacked.
*Repair:* state the strongest version of the opposing view, then audit that.

**Affirming the consequent.** "If A then B; B; therefore A."
*Repair:* name the other causes of B.

**Scope jump.** A result from one population, time, or setting is applied to another.
*Repair:* state both scopes and what would need to hold for the transfer.

**Composition / division.** Properties of parts asserted of the whole, or vice versa.
*Repair:* name the interaction the transfer ignores.

## Probability failures

**Regression to the mean.** An extreme measurement is treated as a real change.
*Repair:* ask whether the follow-up measurement is extreme in the same way, and whether a control group also changed.

**Multiple comparisons.** Many tests are run; the one that reached significance is reported.
*Repair:* ask how many comparisons were made and whether the finding was pre-specified.

**Conditional probability inversion.** P(A|B) is treated as P(B|A) — "90% of cases test positive" becomes "90% of positives are cases".
*Repair:* state both conditionals with their denominators. The base rate does the rest.

**Binary thinking about continuous evidence.** One failed test treated as refutation; one success as proof.
*Repair:* use likelihood language — how much more probable is the evidence under one hypothesis than the other.

## Rhetorical failures (content-free persuasion)

**Significance inflation.** A small effect described as transformative.
*Repair:* restate with the effect size.

**Loaded framing.** A neutral fact described in evaluative words.
*Repair:* restate in neutral terms and see whether the argument survives.

**Emotional appeal substituting for evidence.** Urgency, outrage, or sympathy doing the work of a premise.
*Repair:* extract the factual claim, then audit it on its own.

**Repetition as support.** The same point asserted several times.
*Repair:* count it as one piece of evidence, not several.

## Repair rule

After finding a failure, always write the sentence that states **what the evidence does support**. Narrowing is the deliverable; the label is not.
