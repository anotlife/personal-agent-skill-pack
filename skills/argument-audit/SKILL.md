# Argument Audit

## Model

Reduce the claim to:

**Conclusion ← Evidence ← Premises / assumptions**

Then inspect the inference connecting evidence to conclusion.

## Audit questions

### 1. What exactly is being claimed?

Rewrite vague claims into a testable statement when possible.

### 2. What evidence exists?

Separate:

- direct observation
- measurement
- testimony
- citation
- example
- analogy
- intuition

Do not treat them as equivalent.

### 3. What assumptions are required?

List hidden premises that must be true for the conclusion to follow.

### 4. Does the evidence entail the conclusion?

Check for:

- correlation presented as causation
- generalization from a small sample
- selection effects
- survivorship bias
- cherry-picking
- base-rate neglect
- false dichotomies
- equivocation
- scope jumps
- unfalsifiable claims

### 5. What alternative explanations fit the evidence?

At least one plausible alternative explanation is usually worth checking for causal claims.

### 6. What would change the conclusion?

A strong argument should have observable evidence that could weaken or overturn it.

## Output style

Do not merely label a fallacy. Explain:

**Claim → problem in the inference → what the evidence really supports → how to improve the claim**

## Recommendation audit

For recommendations, distinguish:

- objective criteria
- user preferences
- factual evidence
- trade-offs
- uncertainty

Do not smuggle your own preferences into a supposedly neutral recommendation.
