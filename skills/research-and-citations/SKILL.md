# Research and Citations

## Progressive loading

1. Use the core rules below for ordinary research.
2. Read `references/search-routing.md` when the topic is broad, current, or hard to find.
3. Read `references/clarifying-questions.md` before asking the user anything — or before launching a long research run with an ambiguous brief.
4. Read `references/source-quality-and-triangulation.md` when evidence quality decides the answer.
5. Read `references/citation-audit.md` before finalizing research-heavy prose.
6. Read `references/recency-and-conflict.md` when timing matters or sources disagree.
7. Read `references/copyright-and-quoting.md` before quoting or summarizing third-party text at length.
8. Weak model: read `references/weak-model-mode.md` first.

## Core rules

- **Search for evidence, not decoration.** Search when freshness, niche knowledge, explicit verification, or source-specific evidence matters. Do not search to sprinkle citations over stable general knowledge.
- **Prefer primary and first-party sources** when they directly answer the question.
- **Separate what sources establish from your own synthesis.** Mark the boundary visibly.
- **Cite consequential externally sourced claims near the claim**, inline, not in a trailing list the reader must map back.
- **Never fabricate** references, URLs, DOIs, authors, dates, journal names, page numbers, or quotations. A guessed citation is worse than no citation.
- **Honour the quote budget.** Keep quotations short and few; longer material gets original summary, not reconstruction.
- **Distinguish publication date from event date.** A new article can repeat an old fact.
- **Preserve disagreement.** Do not average incompatible evidence into a fake consensus.
- **Stop when the evidence is sufficient** to support the requested conclusion, and important conflicts have been checked.
- **Say what you could not establish.** A named gap is a result; a filled-in guess is a defect.

## Claim-to-source discipline

For each consequential statement, be able to answer: which exact source, supporting which exact claim, at what scope, from when. If any of those four is missing, either find it or downgrade the statement.

## Final gate

- Every externally sourced claim has a source that actually supports it at the stated scope.
- No citation was written from memory.
- Quotations are short, attributed, and few.
- Timing-sensitive claims state the relevant date.
- Unresolved conflicts and unverified gaps are disclosed, not smoothed over.
