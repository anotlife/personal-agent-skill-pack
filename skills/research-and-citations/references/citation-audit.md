# Citation Audit

For every consequential externally sourced statement ask:

1. What exact claim am I making?
2. Which source supports that exact claim?
3. Does the source support the whole claim or only part of it?
4. Is the citation close enough for the reader to map claim to evidence?
5. Did I accidentally add an inference as though it came from the source?

## Common failures

- citation laundering: a source is cited for a broader claim than it supports;
- stale citation: old evidence is presented as current;
- source mismatch: secondary summary used when primary source is available;
- invented metadata: DOI, author, year, page, or URL guessed from memory;
- citation drift: one citation at paragraph end appears to support several unrelated sentences.

## Final table

For important research answers, an internal claim→source table is useful even if it is not shown to the user.
