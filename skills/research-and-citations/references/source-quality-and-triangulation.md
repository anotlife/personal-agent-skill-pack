# Source Quality and Triangulation

## Practical preference order

1. primary documents / datasets / original research;
2. official documentation / first-party statements;
3. reputable secondary analysis;
4. community reports when lived experience is the relevant evidence;
5. low-authority aggregators only when no better source exists, and with caution.

## Independence test

Ten pages repeating the same announcement are not ten independent confirmations.

Look for different underlying evidence, authorship, institutions, datasets, or direct measurements.

## Source-function matching

A source can be authoritative for one question and weak for another. For example, official documentation is strong for intended API behavior but may be poor evidence of real-world reliability.

## Synthesis

When sources disagree:

- preserve the disagreement;
- identify what each source actually measured or claimed;
- check dates and scope;
- prefer the source closest to the disputed fact;
- do not “average” incompatible evidence into a fake consensus.
