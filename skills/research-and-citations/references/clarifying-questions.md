# Clarifying Questions Before Research

## Principle

One good question costs seconds. Wrong research costs a full run and, worse, gets presented with confidence. But a useless question wastes a turn and annoys the user. Ask rarely, and ask well.

## When to ask

Ask **only** when the missing piece would send the research in a materially different direction — usually because a term is ambiguous or the scope is undefined.

| Ask | Do not ask |
|---|---|
| A core term has two readings with different answers ("fastest growing" = absolute or percentage?) | Anything the user already said |
| Scope is genuinely undefined (time window, region, population) and different choices give different answers | Anything you can default safely and note |
| Two incompatible deliverables are plausible | Anything discoverable from context |
| A constraint changes the method (only open-access sources? only peer-reviewed?) | Anything generic ("what depth do you want?") |

Do **not** ask when a reasonable default exists. Pick it, launch, and state the assumption in the answer. Only ask when the answer would send the research elsewhere.

## How to ask

1. **Never more than three questions.**
2. Number them.
3. Make each answerable in a few words — ideally two options plus a default.
4. State your recommended default so a one-word reply works.
5. Drop anything that is merely interesting.

Good shape:

> Two things would change the answer:
> 1. Absolute revenue growth or percentage growth? (default: absolute)
> 2. All companies, or startups under 10 years old? (default: all)

Bad shape:

> Could you tell me more about what you're looking for? What sources should I use? How long should the report be? What's the deadline?

## Ask once, then proceed

- Ask your questions, then **stop and wait**. Do not ask and launch in the same turn.
- Once the user replies, launch immediately. Do not ask a second round.
- If the user's reply is partial, use defaults for the rest and state them.
- If the user says "just do it", proceed with your defaults and disclose them.

## State assumptions instead of asking

When a default is safe and conventional, take it and record it in one line inside the answer: "assuming a five-year window, since none was specified". A stated assumption is cheap; a question is expensive; a silent assumption is dangerous.

## Preserve the brief

When handing the research to a sub-agent or a long run, pass the user's requirements through verbatim — original question, scope, required or excluded sources, format, depth. Paraphrasing drops constraints silently.

## Checklist

- [ ] I am asking at most three questions.
- [ ] Each question changes the research if answered differently.
- [ ] Each question has a stated default, so a one-word reply suffices.
- [ ] I am not asking anything already answered or discoverable.
- [ ] I will wait for the reply before launching.
