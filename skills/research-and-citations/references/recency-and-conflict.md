# Recency and Conflict

## Two dates, always

When "latest", "current", "today", "recent", or any changing state matters, track two distinct dates:

- **Source date** — when the page, report, or dataset was published or last updated.
- **State/event date** — when the underlying event happened or the fact became true.

These differ constantly. A 2026 article can report a 2019 fact. A 2024 filing can describe a 2018 policy that is still in force. A page updated yesterday can carry text written five years ago.

Report the state date for the claim, and the source date for the evidence.

## Freshness rules

- Never assume the newest-looking result is the newest fact.
- Never assume an old source is stale — a law, a standard, or a definition may not have changed.
- For a "current" question, find the most recent authoritative source you can open, not the most recently published page on the topic.
- Watch for syndication dates: an outlet republishing an old wire story shows a new date on old content.
- If the user's question implies "as of now" and your newest evidence is months old, say how old it is.

## Ordering evidence when dates conflict

1. Identify the **exact disputed proposition**. Vague disputes cannot be resolved.
2. Compare source dates, then state dates.
3. Go to the primary or first-party source if it exists.
4. Check whether the sources are talking about different time windows, populations, or definitions — this resolves more conflicts than ranking sources does.
5. Report the remaining disagreement.

## Preserving disagreement

Do not:

- average incompatible figures into a middle number;
- present the more convenient source as settled;
- drop the conflicting source silently;
- hedge with "reports vary" without saying which reports and how.

Do:

- state both positions with their dates and sources;
- say what would resolve it;
- say which you would trust more and why — one sentence, not a paragraph.

## Self-preference

When your own memory conflicts with a source you just read, the source wins. When two sources conflict and one is your earlier answer in this session, re-check it rather than defending it. Changing a conclusion on new evidence is the correct behaviour, not a failure.

## Common traps

| Trap | Correction |
|---|---|
| "As of my knowledge cutoff..." used to avoid searching | Search when the question is freshness-sensitive |
| Citing a page's "last updated" as the fact's date | Distinguish the two dates |
| Treating a version number as current | Check the release page |
| Assuming a policy is still in force | Check for a repeal or superseding document |
| Assuming a price or quota is current | Prices and quotas change without a new headline |
| Confusing "announced" with "available" | Announcement date ≠ availability date |

## Checklist

- [ ] Every time-sensitive claim carries a state date.
- [ ] Every source's own date is known and is different from the state date where relevant.
- [ ] Conflicting sources were reported, not smoothed.
- [ ] I said where my evidence is older than the user might assume.
