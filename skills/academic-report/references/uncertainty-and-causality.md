# Uncertainty and Causality

## The claim ladder

Use the strongest statement the evidence permits — and no stronger.

| Rung | Form | Requires |
|---|---|---|
| **Observation** | "X increased from A to B in the recorded run." | The record |
| **Derived result** | "Computing Y from the recorded values by METHOD gives Z." | A stated method |
| **Supported interpretation** | "The result is consistent with X contributing to Y." | A mechanism plus a design that could have shown otherwise |
| **Tentative explanation** | "One possible explanation is X; another is Z." | Named alternatives |
| **Causal claim** | "X caused Y." | A design that identifies causality: randomisation, a control, or a natural experiment |

Most student reports need rungs 1-4. Rung 5 requires more than a correlation.

## The overreach patterns

| Pattern | Example | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Correlation → causation | "Temperature rose with yield, so temperature drives yield" | "Yield rose with temperature; other factors were not controlled" |
| One run → a rule | "The enzyme is inactive above 40 °C" | "In this run, activity was not detected at 40 °C" |
| Small sample → population | "Most users prefer X (n=5)" | "All five respondents preferred X" |
| Expected mechanism → demonstrated mechanism | "The colour change shows the reaction completed" | "The colour change is consistent with completion; extent was not measured" |
| Missing control → strong attribution | "The treatment improved output" | "Output was higher after treatment; without a control, the improvement cannot be attributed to it" |
| Significance → importance | "The difference was significant (p = 0.03)" | Add the effect size: "a 0.4% increase, statistically detectable at n=10,000" |
| Absence of evidence → evidence of absence | "No effect was found, so X has no effect" | "No effect was detectable at this sample size; a smaller effect is not excluded" |
| Post-hoc reasoning | Picking the one subgroup that agreed | Report the pre-specified comparison, or label the finding exploratory |

## The falsification test

For each interpretation in the Discussion, ask: *what measurement would show this is wrong?* If nothing could, the sentence is not an interpretation of the data — it is a restatement of your prior belief.

Include that measurement in the "next check" slot. Reports that name the discriminating experiment read as more competent than reports that overclaim.

## Precision of hedging

Hedging must carry information, not just soften.

| Weak | Better |
|---|---|
| "It might possibly suggest that perhaps..." | "This is consistent with H" |
| "The results were quite good" | "The measured error was 3.1%, within the 5% target" |
| "There may be some limitations" | "The main limitation is that only one temperature was tested, so the trend across temperatures is not established" |

Stacked hedges remove information and read as uncertainty about facts rather than about inference. Hedge the inference, not the measurement.

## Writing the Discussion for a surprising result

1. State the result plainly.
2. Say why it is surprising: what was expected, and on what basis.
3. Give 2-3 candidate explanations, in order of plausibility.
4. For each, state the evidence for and against.
5. Name the measurement that would discriminate.
6. State the limitation of the current design.

Do not resolve the surprise by inventing a mechanism with no support, and do not bury it as an "anomaly" in one clause.

## Reporting null and negative results

A null result is a result. Report:

- what was tested, with the sample size and the detectable effect size;
- what was observed;
- whether the design had the power to detect the effect at all.

"This was not detectable at n = 6; the design could only detect effects larger than X" is a real finding. "No conclusion can be drawn" is usually false laziness.

## Checklist

- [ ] Every causal word is supported by a design that could identify causality.
- [ ] Every generalisation names its sample and its scope.
- [ ] Hedges carry information rather than softening.
- [ ] Each interpretation names what would falsify it.
- [ ] Null and negative results are reported with their power.
