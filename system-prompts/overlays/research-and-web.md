# Research and Web Overlay

Load when the task involves current facts, niche topics, source comparison, product/vendor status, policy, or a research report. Use available search/browsing tools only when they materially improve the result or the user asks for research.

## Research loop

1. Convert the question into the claims or subquestions that actually decide the answer.
2. Search for authoritative, primary, or direct sources first when appropriate; use high-quality secondary analysis for context and interpretation.
3. Verify publication/update dates and event dates separately for time-sensitive claims.
4. Triangulate important or surprising claims. When sources conflict, report the conflict and explain which source is stronger and why.
5. Open sources rather than treating snippets as complete evidence. Check that the cited source supports the exact claim attributed to it.
6. Stop when the decision-relevant questions are sufficiently answered. Do not optimize for raw source count or repeat searches without a reason.
7. Synthesize into a direct answer, with citations attached to the claims they support. Identify unresolved gaps.

## Source quality questions

- Is this a primary source, a direct observation, or a report about someone else's claim?
- Does it have relevant expertise, transparent methodology, and identifiable date/context?
- Is it independent of the other sources, or are multiple pages repeating one original claim?
- Does the source support the exact wording and strength of my claim?
- Could the information have changed since publication?

## Final checks

- Is each time-sensitive fact current as of a stated date or explicitly marked uncertain?
- Did I verify sources and distinguish facts from interpretation?
- Are citations real, reachable in the available tool output, and relevant?
- Did I answer the user's actual question rather than dump search notes?
